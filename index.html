Threat model

The useful unit is a conduct warning linked to a minimum viable partial marker—not a public dossier or universal rating. Combined details can still identify people, so minimization must account for the whole report.

  • No legal names, faces, exact addresses, full numbers, IDs, or active locations.
  • No bulk export, public indexing, global identity graph, or ad network.
  • Search favors explicit partial markers instead of narrative mining.
  • Positive notes cannot negate independent warnings.

Posting rules

  1. Share only what another worker needs for a screening or safety decision.
  2. Separate observed behavior from inference or identity speculation.
  3. Never identify another worker without clear, revocable consent.
  4. High-risk reports require human review in a real deployment.
  5. Retaliatory, discriminatory, promotional, or intentionally false reports are removed.

NO PUBLIC API  ·  NO BULK EXPORT  ·  NO UNIVERSAL SCORE

This build implements a real single community node: a zero-dependency Node + SQLite backend enforces the privacy checks and 90-day retention server-side, mints a one-time revocation receipt per report, records lifecycle actions, offers optional AES-256-GCM encryption at rest for report narratives, provides an authenticated moderator role with a human review queue (high-risk bulletins, contests, corrections, and emergency-unpublish requests are resolved by a moderator, every resolution recorded), and supports signed, opt-in federation: a node signs its own bulletins with an Ed25519 key, and peers pinned in data/peers.json pull and verify them into read-only mirrors, with revocations and removals propagated as signed tombstones. It does not yet implement end-to-end encryption in transit, or reporter pseudonymous accounts (reporters stay account-less by design). A multi-node production system also needs protocol design, governance, jurisdiction-specific legal analysis, device security, and adversarial testing.

Retention

The demo models a 90-day expiry for locally created reports. Expiry removes the local demo record when the browser next loads. A production policy must define what can renew a report, how emergency deletion propagates, and what minimal audit evidence remains.

Correction, contest, revocation, and emergency unpublish

These are separate actions. A correction does not increase corroboration. A reporter needs a private recovery receipt to edit or revoke their report. A subject needs a privacy-preserving contest channel. Emergency unpublishing should propagate before an appeal is resolved.

Urgent support

LilithList is not emergency dispatch and should not imply that police contact is safe, wanted, or available. Production nodes should maintain community-governed mutual-aid and crisis resources, show the last verification date, and never infer a worker’s location without explicit consent.

HUMAN REVIEW / SINGLE NODE

moderation

Moderators resolve the review queue: high-risk bulletins awaiting review, contested reports, correction requests, and emergency unpublish requests. Every resolution is recorded.

MODERATOR ACCESS. Moderators are the only accountable role on the node. Reporters remain account-less. The bootstrap key is printed once in the server log on first run; set MOD_BOOTSTRAP_KEY to choose it.
